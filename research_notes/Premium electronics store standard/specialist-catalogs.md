# Specialist electronics catalog buying experience

Observed 19 October 2026 unless a source gives its own date. Stock counts and prices below are point-in-time page contents, not standing inventory. DigiKey’s US product HTML was blocked by a bot check on fetch; DigiKey facts below come from indexed product-page text and DigiKey’s own help posts. Full checkout flows were not completed on any site.

## What does each site put on the product page before a buyer will add a known part?

### Takeaway
Engineers add a known part only after they can confirm identity (manufacturer part number, seller part number, package), a real stock quantity or a dated replenishment, a price for the quantity they need, and a datasheet or equivalent spec they trust more than the photo. Maker shops (Adafruit, SparkFun, Seeed) replace the parametric wall with plain-language limits, pin-level caveats, and a tutorial; volume distributors put the same facts in a spec table, a price ladder, and a datasheet link.

### Cited Findings
- Baymard’s May 2024 study of B2B electronic components (168 sessions with engineers, purchasers, and manufacturing professionals; distributors included Arrow, DigiKey, Farnell/Newark/element14, Mouser, and RS) says product pages must provide datasheets, pricing tables, shipping and stock status, compatible-parts information, and basics such as one or two images and a detailed description. Missing any of these often made participants abandon the product, and sometimes the site, when the gap felt sitewide. The same study says the page must serve both the buyer who already knows the part number and wants inventory and price immediately, and the buyer who needs specs, the manufacturer datasheet, and CAD or other downloads. — [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch)
- A biomedical engineer in that testing would not buy a force-sensing resistor at CoreStaff because there was no datasheet, and went to another distributor that had one. An electronics engineer at element14 did not trust the product photo of a spring-loaded connector and opened the datasheet for a dimensioned drawing in millimetres and inches. A senior electrical engineer at ST’s store looked for the stock count first and said that 100 in stock versus 100,000 would change how many he bought. An electronics engineer at Arrow chose a semiconductor with 1,000 in stock over a cheaper one with 30. — [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch)

### Inferences
- For an LM7805-class part, the pre-cart checklist that these catalogs actually surface is: exact manufacturer and part number, package (TO-220, tube versus loose), output voltage and current, a datasheet, a numeric stock figure or a date, unit price at quantity 1 and at the next breaks, and whether the photo is generic. Lifecycle (Active versus Obsolete) and a named substitute matter as soon as the exact part is unavailable.
- For an HC-SR04-class module, Adafruit and SparkFun show that the extra facts that close the sale are electrical compatibility (5 V echo versus 3.3 V), what is in the bag, dimensions, and a worked example. Seeed puts that same material on a wiki with schematic and version history rather than only in a short store blurb.
- A small premium shop can meet the Baymard “both speeds” requirement with one calm block: part number, price, stock, and Add to cart stay visible, and the datasheet, pinout, and caveats sit immediately under them. It does not need DigiKey’s substitute matrix or Arrow’s regional inventory cards to do that.

### Gaps
- DigiKey.com itself was not rendered past Cloudflare, so US-site layout, cart, and checkout were not seen.
- Newark and element14 product-page HTML for an LM7805 was not fetched; Newark evidence is the help center plus Baymard’s 2024 session notes about element14 and RS.
- Seeed’s current store template (price, stock, add to cart) was not successfully fetched for the ultrasonic module.
- No ESP32 product page was opened. Patterns above are from regulators and ultrasonic modules, plus Seeed’s module-packaging posts.
- Guest checkout was not walked on any of the nine sites, so payment steps, account walls, and order-review screens are not described from observation.

## How do search, part-number lookup, and filters work, and what matters at a few dozen parts?

### Takeaway
At distributor scale, buyers either paste a part number or filter a huge table by one electrical parameter at a time, then sort by price or stock. Baymard’s 2024 testing found that every participant filtered or tried to, and that engineers preferred DigiKey’s filters above the table because a left sidebar stole columns. A catalog of dozens of parts does not need that machinery. It does need exact part-number hit, a short category list, and a few columns that differ between the parts you actually sell.

### Cited Findings
- DigiKey’s own part-search article says a keyword such as “resistors” returns category hits, and choosing “Through Hole Resistors” opened a set of more than 501,000 items. Filters can be horizontal (“scrolling”) or vertical (“stacked”). The article tells the reader to apply the most important parameter first (resistance), that multiple values in one field can be clicked or dragged as a range, and that filtering several parameters at once can return zero parts, so one parameter at a time is safer. The article says DigiKey keeps adding parametric features and will revise the page; it does not give a publication date in the fetched text, so treat the 501,000 figure as DigiKey’s own example, not a 2026 census. — [DigiKey, How to Use DigiKey Part Search](https://www.digikey.se/en/articles/how-to-use-digi-key-part-search)
- DigiKey’s public Product Information API separates keyword search, part-number search (manufacturer or DigiKey number, including partial, with “MyPricing” when it applies), product details for one match, alternate packaging, and Digi-Reel pricing. That is a part-number-first lookup model, not a browse model. — [DigiKey developer portal, ProductSearch](https://developer.digikey.com/products/product-information-v4/productsearch)
- In Baymard’s 2024 electronics testing, 100% of participants filtered or tried to filter the product table. Sorting by price and availability was described by a participant as the two most used sort options, and was used as a soft alternative to a hard filter. Participants at ST disliked a left-hand filter column because it consumed table width. Participants at DigiKey praised filters across the top; one narrowed 1,300+ microcontrollers to 4 that way. DigiKey’s sort arrows were understood once a tooltip said what “up” would do; at Microchip Direct, arrow direction was misread. — [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch)
- The same study says desktop product tables beat a list view for spec-heavy parts because more attributes can be scanned across rows. Failures it recorded: a horizontal scrollbar only visible after vertical scrolling (Microchip), off-screen columns so an engineer opened datasheets instead (Future Electronics), and a sticky scrollbar at DigiKey that a design engineer could find. On mobile, Baymard recommends a list, not a table. It only tested desktop, and says the tested B2B electronics sites averaged 73% of monthly traffic from desktop, some as high as 83%. — [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch); [Baymard product tables, 10 Apr 2024](https://baymard.com/research-articles/use-product-tables-for-desktop-product-listings)
- Baymard’s 10 April 2024 article says list view on desktop makes specialized parts hard to compare, and that product tables should be the desktop listing for this category. Its public example set includes annotated tables from DigiKey, Mouser, Newark, RS, Arrow, TTI, Avnet, and others. — [Baymard, 10 Apr 2024](https://baymard.com/research-articles/use-product-tables-for-desktop-product-listings); [Baymard product-table examples](https://baymard.com/ecommerce-design-examples/product-table)
- Baymard’s 23 April 2024 article says category filters often contain 100+ options (its example is resistance values for surface-mount resistors) and that a search box inside the filter should appear when a filter has more than about 15–20 options. 46% of the test sites did not let users reliably search within filter types. A participant at Arrow searched “1μF” and cut 44,000+ capacitors to 777. Suggestions should show match counts, be clearable, and highlight the unmatched part of the suggestion. — [Baymard, 23 Apr 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/allow-search-for-long-filter-options-lists)
- Baymard’s industry collection page, covering 17 components-and-machinery sites scored on 429 elements, states that compatible parts, substitutes, and alternatives were almost entirely unsupported across the industry. Numeric scores are paywalled. — [Baymard benchmark collection](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/collections/components-machinery)
- An older RS export microsite says search accepts name, brand, stock number, and MPN; list pages show live stock and add to cart; the cart has a Quick Order area for typing or pasting stock numbers in bulk. The page is undated marketing copy on export.rsdelivers.com, so treat it as RS’s stated ordering model, not a 2026 audit. — [RS, Ordering Online](https://export.rsdelivers.com/campaigns/microsites/ordering_online)
- Mouser’s Price and Availability Assistant accepts manual part numbers (Mouser or manufacturer), paste, or a spreadsheet of up to 200 lines (.xls, .xlsx, .csv, .txt). If quantity is omitted it defaults to 1. Results can be added to a project. The BOM tool shows a match-confidence score, description, both part numbers, RoHS, image, up to five quantities per line, packaging choices, and unit plus extended price, and says the quote is guaranteed for 30 days after processing. — [Mouser Price and Availability Assistant](https://www.mouser.com/help/tools/how-to-use-the-price-and-availability-assistant); [Mouser BOM matches](https://www.mouser.com/help/tools/view-bom-matches)
- RS product pages include “find similar products” by ticking attributes already on the part, which is a filter entered from a known part rather than from a million-row category. — [RS Hong Kong, LM7805CT/NOPB](https://hken.rs-online.com/web/p/voltage-regulators/7968060)
- NN/g’s 9 February 2024 article on comparison tables says comparison is often required before a purchase, that the attributes are usually scattered across detail pages, and that a comparison table works for a small number of offerings when content is consistent, scannable, and simply laid out. — [NN/g, 9 Feb 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/comparison-tables/)
- NN/g’s 23 August 2024 update of Jakob Nielsen’s 2013 article says that when differences between options are implied, people pick the wrong one, and that experts on the design team overestimate what is obvious. — [NN/g, 23 Aug 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/explicit-differences/)
- Baymard’s general product-list benchmark, updated 9 September 2025, is about broad ecommerce, not electronics: 58% of desktop sites and 78% of mobile sites were “poor” to “mediocre” on product-list UX, across 170+ sites. One of its eight practices is to combine variations into one list item (42% don’t). That variation guidance is illustrated with apparel, so it is a weak fit for distinct part numbers. — [Baymard, updated 9 Sep 2025](https://baymard.com/research-articles/current-state-product-list-and-filtering)

### Inferences
- Patterns that still matter at a few dozen parts: a search box that treats the manufacturer part number, your own SKU, and a plain name (“7805”, “ultrasonic”, “nano”) as first-class queries; a result that is the product, not a category of 501,000; and a listing that shows the three or four attributes a buyer uses to tell your parts apart (voltage, package, logic level, interface).
- Patterns that are for million-SKU catalogs: horizontal parametric bars with hundreds of resistance values, search-inside-filter, one-parameter-at-a-time warnings, BOM upload for 200 lines, and separate TR/CT/DKR part numbers. Baymard’s own threshold (search inside a filter only past about 15–20 options) implies a 40-part shop can show every value in a short list.
- A comparison table, in the NN/g 2024 sense, fits a small shop better than a DigiKey table: a handful of regulators or sensors, same attributes on every row, differences written out (5 V fixed versus adjustable, 5 V echo versus 3.3 V).
- Do not merge distinct part numbers into one “color swatch” list item. Baymard’s 2025 variation rule is for shirts. An LM7805 and an AMS1117 are different parts; hiding that is how buyers order the wrong footprint.

### Gaps
- Autocomplete behavior, zero-results pages, and synonym handling were not exercised live. Baymard’s DigiKey case study lists “Autocomplete Suggestions” and “No Results Page” as reviewed page types, but the scores and screenshots are paywalled. — [Baymard DigiKey case study](https://baymard.com/ux-benchmark/case-studies/digikey)
- LCSC’s parametric search UI was not opened; only product-detail pages were captured.
- Adafruit and SparkFun search and category pages were not captured.

## How do they show price, MOQ, quantity breaks, stock, and shipping without looking cheap?

### Takeaway
The distributors that look serious put a single unit price next to a numeric stock count, then a quiet table of quantity, unit price, and extended price. They separate “in stock now” from “on order, dated” and from “factory lead time.” Pack quantity and order multiple are stated as facts, not as a promotion. Maker shops often show one price and “In stock,” and put shipping rules on a policy page rather than in a badge row. Clutter comes from multiple regional buy boxes, pack-versus-piece math, and sale iconography, not from the price ladder itself.

### Cited Findings
- DigiKey’s tactile-switch ladder is three columns (quantity, unit price, extended price), split by packaging, with the reel fee in a single footnote and tariffs deferred to the cart. The LM7805 UK snippet shows unit price ex-VAT and inc-VAT, not a crossed-out “was” price. — [DigiKey, TL3330AF130QG](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/e-switch/TL3330AF130QG/1426835); [DigiKey UK, LM7805CT/NOPB](https://www.digikey.co.uk/en/products/detail/texas-instruments/LM7805CT-NOPB/3901929)
- Mouser’s product page uses the same quantity / unit / extended table and states minimum and multiple beside the stock count. Its category rows show the break prices in the row, so a buyer can compare without opening the product. Incoming stock is a count plus a date, not a vague “available.” — [Mouser, TL52AF260Q](https://www.mouser.com/ProductDetail/612-TL52AF260Q); [Mouser UK category table](https://www.mouser.co.uk/c/semiconductors/amplifier-ics/)
- In Baymard’s 2024 testing, an RS user had to add an out-of-stock logic gate to the cart to learn the replenishment date. A Mouser user opened “View Dates” on the table and saw ship dates plus factory lead time for quantities beyond stock and on-order, and treated a date later this month or next month as acceptable for his project. — [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch)
- LCSC prints “Minimum: 20 Multiple: 20” (or 5, or 1) on the same line as the stock count, then the ladder including the extended amount at each break, then “Standard Packaging.” That is clear and also visibly a volume wholesaler: the first row is often not quantity 1. — [LCSC C347478](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C347478.html); [LCSC C561480](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C561480.html)
- RS shows both per-unit and per-pack prices, and the pack size changes by region (5 pieces versus a tube of 45). The Dutch page adds “bulk discount available” and a free-delivery threshold. Stock is split into “ready to ship,” “ready from another location,” and “shipping from [date].” — [RS Norway](https://no.rs-online.com/web/p/voltage-regulators/7968060); [RS New Zealand](https://nz.rs-online.com/web/p/voltage-regulators/9222369); [RS Netherlands snippet](https://nl.rs-online.com/web/p/voltage-regulators/7968060)
- Newark documents tiered pricing, time-limited prices marked with icons, contract-price icons, and a quote form for the lowest large-quantity price. Same-day shipment is tied to a cutoff, and over-stock quantities become a backorder rather than a hard stop. — [Newark help](https://www.newark.com/help-pricing-availability)
- Arrow’s indexed L7805CV buy area repeats price and ship promise once per region, with different unit prices, and invites login for better pricing. The no-stock part switches the button to Quote and states a 4,000-piece minimum. — [Arrow, L7805CV](https://www.arrow.com/en/products/l7805cv/stmicroelectronics); [Arrow, NCP81270MNTXG](https://www.arrow.com/en/products/ncp81270mntxg/on-semiconductor)
- Adafruit’s HC-SR04 is a single $3.95 with “In stock,” no ladder. SparkFun’s HC-SR04 is a single $5.25 with “In stock,” no ladder in the fetch. Adafruit’s shipping page says orders usually ship in 1–2 business days; orders placed by 11:00 ET on weekdays with DHL Express, UPS 3 Day, 2nd Day, or Next Day usually ship the same day; tracking is emailed. It lists carrier, order-value caps (USPS Ground Advantage and Priority only under $200; UPS Ground Saver under $500), battery restrictions, dimensional weight, and that DHL options appear at checkout when the destination qualifies. It does not put those rules on the product page that was fetched. — [Adafruit HC-SR04](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3942); [SparkFun HC-SR04](https://www.sparkfun.com/products/15569); [Adafruit shipping](https://www.adafruit.com/shipping)
- Baymard, 27 June 2023, says 41% of sites in its general ecommerce benchmark gave shipping speed (“2 business days”) rather than a delivery date, and that participants stopped to calculate arrival themselves, including weekends and cutoffs. The article is not electronics-specific. Its method for a date is: current date and cutoff, processing time (including stock and restock), then transit time. — [Baymard, 27 Jun 2023](https://baymard.com/research-articles/shipping-speed-vs-delivery-date)
- An unofficial Arrow PDP critique (a designer’s static page, not Arrow and not Baymard) argues that showing In-stock, backorder, and request-quote as separate inventory buckets is confusing, and that the spec block pushes the buy box below the fold. It asserts a “large abandonment rate” without a source. Use it only as a description of the multi-bucket pattern, not as a measured result. — [Product Detail Page @ Arrow](https://costanza14.github.io/pdp.html)

### Inferences
- The uncluttered pattern is: one price for one piece, the stock count in the same cluster, breaks in a small table with extended price so the buyer sees the line total, and a single sentence for how it ships (“in stock, ships tomorrow” or “12 on hand, more on [date]”). VAT or tax is either included or explicitly excluded, not both in a badge row.
- MOQ and multiple should be visible before Add to cart, as LCSC and Mouser do. For a premium shop selling singles, MOQ 1 and multiple 1 can be stated once, not repeated as a wholesaler banner.
- Do not copy RS’s per-pack and per-piece pair unless you truly sell a sealed pack. Do not copy Arrow’s stacked regional offers. Do not copy Newark’s special-price icon set. Those solve account pricing and multi-warehouse inventory.
- A free-shipping threshold and carrier menu belong at cart or on a shipping page, as Adafruit does, unless the promise is flat and simple enough to say once under the price.

### Gaps
- The oft-cited Baymard figure that many sites omit estimated shipping cost on the product page was not re-fetched with its publication date, so it is not used here.
- Cart totals, tax calculation, and the moment shipping cost appears were not observed, except Adafruit’s written policy and DigiKey’s note that tariffs show up in the cart.
- Whether Mouser or DigiKey show a delivery date at checkout in 2026 was not verified.

## Which trust, documentation, and packaging cues separate a serious parts seller from a gadget shop?

### Takeaway
Serious sellers let the buyer verify the part without trusting the photo: datasheet or schematic, package and pinout, lifecycle or version, a real quantity, and an honest limit (logic level, hot-plug, representative image). Maker specialists add a tutorial and name what is in the bag. Volume distributors add traceability cues (date code, country of origin, reel versus cut tape, RoHS, ECCN). Gadget-shop patterns these sites avoid on the part itself are lifestyle claims without electrical limits, and stock photos presented as the exact component.

### Cited Findings
- Baymard 2024: no datasheet stopped a purchase; a photo was not trusted until the datasheet drawing matched the part number and dimensions; stock quantity was often the first thing sought; lifecycle and lead time decide whether a part fits a design schedule. Attributes missing on some rows and present on others made comparison fail. — [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch)
- DigiKey, Mouser, LCSC, and RS all print a variant of “image is a representation” or “for reference only.” DigiKey and Mouser still link the manufacturer PDF and, for many parts, CAD. — [DigiKey tactile switch](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/e-switch/TL3330AF130QG/1426835); [Mouser TL52AF260Q](https://www.mouser.com/ProductDetail/612-TL52AF260Q); [LCSC](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C347478.html); [RS Hong Kong](https://hken.rs-online.com/web/p/voltage-regulators/7968060)
- Arrow’s L7805CV spec list defines terms in place (what line regulation means, what pin count means) and states compliance and trade fields: RoHS, ECCN, HTS, SVHC, automotive, PPAP, part status, country of origin and date code on the indexed buy cards. — [Arrow, L7805CV](https://www.arrow.com/en/products/l7805cv/stmicroelectronics)
- LCSC’s authenticity line (“100% Authentic · 30-Day Return or Replacement”) sits on the product page next to the ladder. Its other-suppliers flow is different: add to cart to request a quote, quotation by email within 24 working hours. That split (stocked and priced versus quote-only) is itself a trust signal, if the buyer can tell which mode they are in. — [LCSC AO4407](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C6430946.html); [LCSC other suppliers](https://www.lcsc.com/flashSale/landingPage)
- Adafruit’s HC-SR04 page states a narrower “best results” range than the headline range, explains the 5 V echo hazard, and includes the two resistors in the product title. Its learning system attaches guides to product IDs and shows stock and price on the guide. Shipping policy states returns (unopened, 30 days, 5% restocking at $500+, no returns after 6 months), refuses to mark customs forms as gift or sample, and explains VAT/GST by country. — [Adafruit HC-SR04](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3942); [Adafruit Learning System example](https://learn.adafruit.com/products/4440/guides); [Adafruit shipping](https://www.adafruit.com/shipping)
- SparkFun puts datasheet, tutorial, and GitHub in one documentation block on the HC-SR04, and its Learn site publishes hookup guides that name the bootloader, libraries, and what the board can and cannot do. The fetched nRF52840 guide is a tutorial, not a 2026 redesign case study. — [SparkFun HC-SR04](https://www.sparkfun.com/products/15569); [SparkFun Learn, Pro nRF52840 Mini](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/sparkfun-pro-nrf52840-mini-hookup-guide/all)
- Seeed’s ultrasonic wiki records hardware versions and what changed (capacitor, layout, 3.3 V), links schematic and library, and warns that hot-plugging damages the sensor. Its 12 May 2025 post says XIAO boards moved to a reusable clear case with foam after community requests, in three sizes, and that old and new packs might ship during the change. Its 24 October 2025 post (updated 16 January 2026) adds tape-and-reel for named XIAO models, citing pick-and-place, EIA carrier-tape practice, and pocket-level protection, alongside 3-piece packs and pre-soldered headers. — [Seeed wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/Grove-Ultrasonic_Ranger/); [Seeed packaging, 12 May 2025](https://www.seeedstudio.com/blog/2025/05/12/seeed-studio-xiao-gets-a-packaging-upgrade-durable-reusable-and-tiny/); [Seeed tape and reel, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.seeedstudio.com/blog/2025/10/24/seeed-studio-xiao-now-available-in-tape-reel/)
- Mouser and DigiKey treat packaging as part of the part: cut tape versus machine-ready reel, leader length, fee, and ESD/MSL handling. That is the distributor equivalent of “what is in the bag.” — [Mouser packaging](https://www.mouser.com/packaging-reel-options/); [DigiKey TechForum, 28 Nov 2025](https://forum.digikey.com/t/request-for-confirmation-of-packaging-type/62750/2)
- Baymard 2024: two engineers tried to pass an Arrow cart to a buyer or teammate, found no share or saved-list control, and one said he would not use Arrow for that reason. Mouser’s BOM and project tools, and RS’s stated “parts list,” are the counter-pattern: a list a second person can reorder. — [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch); [Mouser BOM](https://www.mouser.com/help/tools/view-bom-matches)
- Older NN/g (2016) says incomplete specs push people to phone support or to Google, and that HTML specs should sit beside the PDF. — [NN/g, 3 July 2016](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-specs/)

### Inferences
- For a small shop selling regulators, sensors, a Nano, and relays, the cues that read as competence are: your SKU and the industry part number both visible; a datasheet or a one-page spec you wrote and stand behind; a photo of the actual item or an explicit representative-image line; package and pinout; logic-level and voltage limits in the first screen; what is included; a numeric stock count; a version or “we ship this maker’s part” note; and a short page on shipping, returns, and what you will not mis-declare.
- Packaging care can be small and physical (a labeled antistatic bag, header pins separate, a card with the pinout) the way Seeed’s case and Adafruit’s included resistors are care. Tape-and-reel is a production cue, not a requirement for a bench-quantity shop.
- Authenticity claims work when they are specific (named manufacturer, datasheet that matches the marking). A bare “100% genuine” line, as on LCSC, is weaker than the datasheet and the part number sitting above it.

### Gaps
- RoHS certificates, lot codes, and actual packing photos were not collected for Adafruit, SparkFun, or Seeed orders. Seeed’s packaging posts are about XIAO boards, not about a loose HC-SR04.
- No 2023–2026 study was found that measures how much a pinout card or antistatic bag changes conversion for a small component shop.

### Takeaway
The only substantial 2023–2026 public research on these exact distributors is Baymard’s B2B electronic-components study and its later benchmarks. Scores and most screenshots are paid. NN/g’s relevant free articles are either general comparison guidance from 2024 or a 2016 spec-page article. The well-known RS redesign writeup is from 2015. No Smashing Magazine or Awwwards teardown of these sites from 2023–2026 was found. Adafruit, SparkFun, and LCSC are outside Baymard’s published distributor set.

### Inferences
- For design decisions, weight Baymard’s 2024 session evidence and the live pages above the 2015 RS case study. The 2015 piece still supports three durable ideas: do not make a known buyer log in to see their price, show stock and price on the product page, and let people save a parts list.
- Paywalled Baymard scores should not be guessed. The public articles already name the failures that matter: hidden restock dates, untrusted photos, missing datasheets, sidebar filters eating the table, and carts that cannot be handed to another person.

### Gaps
- No Awwwards pages, Smashing redesigns, or company engineering-blog postmortems from 2023–2026 were found for DigiKey, Mouser, LCSC, Newark, Arrow, Adafruit, or SparkFun.
- Baymard premium guidelines (the actual 275) were not available, so interface details such as exact filter styling are only known where the public articles quote them.
- The Econsultancy article was not fully fetched; only the opening sections on checkout, pricing, and parts lists were captured via search.

## What should a small shop explicitly not copy from DigiKey-scale catalogs?

### Takeaway
Copy the buying facts (identity, datasheet, numeric stock, dated replenishment, a quiet price ladder, packaging named in one line). Refuse the catalog chrome that exists to manage hundreds of thousands of SKUs, contract pricing, and multi-warehouse allocation. At a few dozen parts, that chrome reads as clutter and hides the care a small shop can actually show.

### Cited Findings
- DigiKey’s resistor example is a category of over 501,000 items, with horizontal and stacked parametric filters and a warning that applying several filters at once can yield zero results. — [DigiKey part-search article](https://www.digikey.se/en/articles/how-to-use-digi-key-part-search)
- DigiKey splits one switch into three orderable part numbers (TR, CT, Digi-Reel) and adds a $7 reel fee. Mouser mirrors that with MouseReel, cut tape, and full-reel multiples. — [DigiKey TL3330AF130QG](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/e-switch/TL3330AF130QG/1426835); [Mouser packaging](https://www.mouser.com/packaging-reel-options/)
- Baymard found left-side filters resented because they stole table columns, and found value in top filters only once the result set was huge (1,300 microcontrollers down to 4; 44,000 capacitors down to 777). It also says search-within-filter is for lists of more than about 15–20 options, and that 100% of its electronics participants were trying to narrow a large table. — [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch); [Baymard, 23 Apr 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/allow-search-for-long-filter-options-lists)
- Arrow’s indexed L7805CV page stacks more than one regional inventory, each with its own price, ship time, date code, and lead time, plus a login prompt for a better price. Its no-stock computing regulator offers Quote at a 4,000-piece minimum. Newark documents contract-price icons and a quote path for the lowest price. — [Arrow L7805CV](https://www.arrow.com/en/products/l7805cv/stmicroelectronics); [Arrow NCP81270MNTXG](https://www.arrow.com/en/products/ncp81270mntxg/on-semiconductor); [Newark help](https://www.newark.com/help-pricing-availability)
- RS’s pack-of-5 versus tube-of-45 pricing, and the 2024 test in which replenishment was only visible after adding to cart, are scale behaviors a small single-warehouse shop can simply not inherit. — [RS New Zealand](https://nz.rs-online.com/web/p/voltage-regulators/9222369); [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch)
- LCSC’s ladders often start at the MOQ (20, or 100 on the third-party capacitor example) and run to tens of thousands, with “Asian Brands” house lines beside franchised-style parts. That price architecture signals bulk sourcing, which fights a premium single-piece position even when the data is honest. — [LCSC C347478](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C347478.html)
- Mouser’s assistant is built for 200-line spreadsheets and five quantities per line. Baymard’s Arrow sessions failed when a cart could not be handed to purchasing. Those are procurement tools. — [Mouser assistant](https://www.mouser.com/help/tools/how-to-use-the-price-and-availability-assistant); [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch)
- Baymard’s mobile note: product tables that work on a wide desktop fail on a phone, and these sites are desktop-heavy (about 73% in their sample). A small shop whose buyers also check a phone between bench sessions should not ship a horizontally scrolling spec grid as the only mobile list. — [Baymard, 8 May 2024](https://baymard.com/blog/b2b-electronic-components-machinery-launch)
- Adafruit and SparkFun already demonstrate the alternative for modules: one price, real photos, limits written in sentences, documentation linked, shipping explained in prose. Seeed’s wiki adds version history and a schematic, which a distributor table does not. — [Adafruit HC-SR04](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3942); [SparkFun HC-SR04](https://www.sparkfun.com/products/15569); [Seeed wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/Grove-Ultrasonic_Ranger/)

### Inferences
- Copy: manufacturer and seller part numbers; one-line function (“5 V, 1.5 A, TO-220”); datasheet; HTML specs for the decisions you actually support; numeric stock; a date when stock is zero; minimum and multiple; a three-column break table if breaks exist; image disclaimer or a photo of your stock; lifecycle or “this is the part we ship”; pinout and electrical hazards in the first screen; a saved list only if two people really share an order.
- Adapt: a desktop comparison table of your actual range (NN/g’s small-set comparison, not a 40-column DigiKey grid); “similar parts” as a short human list (the 3.3 V ultrasonic, the fixed 5 V versus the adjustable) rather than an algorithmic substitute engine; packaging named in a sentence (“loose TO-220 in an antistatic bag”) rather than three SKUs; shipping as a delivery date once you know the destination, per Baymard’s 2023 checkout finding, without a carrier matrix on the product page.
- Refuse: parametric filters for attributes that do not vary in your catalog; search-inside-filter; zero-result filter combinations; separate part numbers per tape style; reel fees; regional price stacks; contract-price icons; quote-only as the default for a part you hold on the shelf; backorder that only reveals its date in the cart; representative photos with no disclaimer; lifestyle copy that omits voltage and logic level; apparel-style variation grouping; a 200-line BOM tool; ECAD for every module; manufacturer lead-time fields you cannot honor; tariff and ECCN blocks unless you actually export that way.

### Gaps
- No published study was found that tests a deliberately small component catalog (under about 100 SKUs) against a DigiKey-style UI, so the refuse list is an inference from Baymard’s large-catalog findings plus the live pages, not a direct experiment.
- Conversion effects of a single price versus a ladder, for a premium component shop, were not found in 2023–2026 sources.
