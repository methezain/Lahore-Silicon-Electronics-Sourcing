# Interface craft for a precise electronics catalog

Research date: 19 October 2026. Scope is transferable craft for a small parts shop (Astro or React), not a copy of Apple, Linear, Stripe, Vercel, or Material visual identity. Official pages are preferred. Third-party measurements of marketing sites are labeled as such and are weaker evidence than a team’s own docs.

## What do current references say about type pairing, optical sizing, tracking, a modular type scale, and tabular numerals for prices and part numbers?

### Takeaway

Publish a short set of named text styles that bundle size, line-height, weight, and tracking. Pair one text face with one smaller monospace for part numbers. Leave optical sizing to the font when it has an opsz axis. Do not letterspace body lowercase; do letterspace all-caps labels. Use tabular lining figures wherever prices or part numbers must align or update in place.

### Cited Findings

- Apple’s system fonts (San Francisco and New York) ship as variable fonts. Optical sizing is the adjustment of letterforms to point size. Dynamic optical sizes merge the old discrete Text and Display cuts, plus weights, into one continuous design so each glyph is interpolated for the size in use. Discrete optical sizes are only needed when a design tool cannot use the variable font. — [Typography, Apple Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography) (body read from a mirror of that page on 19 October 2026; the official URL returned only a title in fetch). The same account of Text versus Display, then variable optical size, is in the WWDC20 transcript. — [The details of UI typography, WWDC20](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10175/)
- Apple: minimize the number of typefaces. Mixing many faces obscures hierarchy and makes the interface feel inconsistent. San Francisco is the sans; New York is a serif designed to work alone and beside SF. SF also has a monospaced variant (SF Mono) and width variants. — [Typography, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- Apple text styles are named bundles of weight, point size, and leading (Large Title, Title 1–3, Headline, Body, Callout, Subheadline, Footnote, Caption). They are the hierarchy, and they scale with Dynamic Type. Leading may be loosened for wide columns and long passages, and tightened in a short list row. Apple says to avoid tight leading when showing three or more lines. In general, avoid Ultralight, Thin, and Light; prefer Regular, Medium, Semibold, or Bold, and go larger if a custom face is thin. — [Typography, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- Apple platform floors (custom fonts should be guided by the same floors): iOS/iPadOS default 17 pt, minimum 11 pt; macOS default 13 pt, minimum 10 pt. macOS Body is Regular 13 pt on 16 pt line height (about 1.23). Headline on macOS is Bold 13/16. — [Typography, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- In a running Apple app, SF adjusts tracking at every point size. Mockups must apply the tracking table because the system will not do it in a static comp. The published macOS and tvOS tables are not a simple “tighter as it gets bigger” rule. Examples in 1/1000 em: 6 pt +41, 11 pt +6, 12 pt 0, 13 pt −6, 17 pt −26, 20 pt −23, 23 pt −4, 24 pt +3, 32 pt +13, 48 pt +8, 72 pt +2, 80 pt and above 0. Apple notes these values are for that resolution and that not every app expresses tracking as 1/1000 em. — [Typography, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- The HIG typography page’s changelog, as mirrored, lists updates on 21 June 2023 (visionOS), 12 September 2023, 10 June 2024, 7 March 2025 (Dynamic Type), and 16 December 2025 (emphasized weights added to Dynamic Type specs). — [Typography, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- WWDC20: SF Text was for sizes below 20 points and SF Display for 20 and above. Text has slightly different vertical proportions so it looks a little larger, and therefore more legible, at the same point size. Spacing between letters was designed differently because one cut is used small and the other large. Variable fonts (OpenType, introduced 2016) made a continuous opsz axis possible; the difference is more visible in New York than in SF Pro. — [The details of UI typography, WWDC20](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10175/)
- Vercel’s Geist typography is consumed as classes that pre-set font-size, line-height, letter-spacing, and font-weight together. Roles are named, not generated from a ratio: headings from `text-heading-72` down through `text-heading-14`; buttons `text-button-16/14/12` (12 only inside an input); labels for single lines (`text-label-20` down to `text-label-12`, plus mono variants); copy for multiple lines, with a higher line-height than labels. `text-copy-24/20/18` are called out for marketing heroes and quotes. `text-copy-14` is “the most commonly used text style.” `text-copy-13` is for secondary text where space is tight. `text-label-13` “uses tabular when conveying numbers.” Mono pairing is explicit: `text-label-14-mono` is the largest mono, to pair with text larger than 14; `text-label-13-mono` pairs with Label 14 because the smaller mono “looks better in that pairing.” Emphasis is a `<strong>` inside the style (Subtle/Strong), not a separate display family. Faces are Geist Sans and Geist Mono. — [Geist Typography](https://vercel.com/geist/typography)
- Stripe Apps do not allow arbitrary font faces in custom styles. Text style is a named `font` token (the docs show `font: 'heading'`). Styling is autocomplete from tokens, not free CSS. — [Style your app, Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/style)
- Butterick’s key rules: the four decisions that determine body text are point size, line spacing, line length, and font. Web body size 15–25 pixels. Line spacing 120–145% of the size (`line-height` without units). Average line length 45–90 characters including spaces, or two to three alphabets. The largest visible improvement is a professional font. Avoid “goofy fonts, monospaced fonts, most free fonts, and system fonts—especially Times New Roman and Arial.” Use bold or italic rarely, and not together. All caps are fine for less than one line. Kerning stays on. — [Summary of key rules, Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html); [Line length](https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html); [Line spacing](https://practicaltypography.com/line-spacing.html)
- Butterick on tracking: do not ordinarily letterspace lowercase. Always add 5–12% extra letterspacing to all caps or small caps (`letter-spacing: 0.05em` to `0.12em`), especially at small sizes. He notes a minority practice of adding letterspacing to lowercase below about 9 point so the gaps stay distinct, and of removing letterspacing from lowercase used large (headlines). If the gaps are wide enough to fit more letters, it has gone too far. — [Letterspacing, Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html)
- Butterick: different fonts at the same point size do not look the same size, so a face that runs small needs less line spacing and the reverse. He also says the common failure of responsive layouts is ignoring line length. — [Line spacing](https://practicaltypography.com/line-spacing.html); [Line length](https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html)
- OpenType `tnum` replaces proportional figures with figures on a uniform width so they can align in a column. `pnum` does the reverse. The spec’s own `tnum` example is oldstyle figures that must align vertically. Tabular width is often the default, but “this cannot be safely assumed.” `tnum` and `pnum` are independent of lining (`lnum`) versus oldstyle (`onum`). — [OpenType feature tags p–t, Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/opentype/otspec140/features_pt)
- CSS `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` maps to `tnum`; `proportional-nums` to `pnum`; `lining-nums` to `lnum`; `oldstyle-nums` to `onum`; `slashed-zero` to `zero` (a slashed 0, “useful when a clear distinction between O and 0 is needed”). The property is widely available since January 2020 and is inherited. — [font-variant-numeric, MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/font-variant-numeric)
- Google Fonts Knowledge (Elliot Jay Stocks): four figure styles — proportional lining, proportional oldstyle, tabular lining, tabular oldstyle. Not every font draws all four; if in doubt, use the designer’s default. Proportional lining is the catch-all for ordinary text. Proportional oldstyle sits better in lowercase prose because ascenders and descenders do not interrupt reading. Tabular lining is for tables, charts, timetables, and any case where figures must line up or may change while staying aligned (the example given is a countdown). Tabular oldstyle is for figures that must line up but still sit inside sentence-case text (the example is ZIP codes in addresses). — [Understanding numerals, Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/knowledge/introducing_type/understanding_numerals)
- Microsoft’s figure design standard: tabular figures share one advance width so columns of data set cleanly. The figure zero is the model for spacing figures and for the advance width of major currency signs (dollar, pound, euro, and others). — [Character design standards: figures, Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/develop/character-design-standards/figures)
- No official public Linear typography specification was found. Linear’s own redesign note (Karri Saarinen, 28 March 2024) talks about reducing visual noise, keeping alignment, and raising hierarchy and density. It does not publish a type scale, tracking table, or pairing. — [How we redesigned the Linear UI (part II)](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)
- Third-party scrapes of linear.app (not Linear’s design system) describe Inter Variable for display, Berkeley Mono for technical text, weights around 400/510/590, and display tracking near −2.2%. Treat those numbers as observations of one marketing page, not as a spec to copy. — [Linear marketing design spec, Tastekit](https://tastekit.dev/apps/linear/marketing/DESIGN.md); [linear.app brand PDF, Designlang](https://www.designlang.app/gallery/linear-app/linear-app.brand.pdf)

### Inferences

- Opinion, for this shop: one text family with a real point of view, plus one mono used only for part numbers, values, and short technical strings. Product descriptions stay in the text face. That follows Apple’s “few faces, and a mono exists for a job” and Geist’s documented mono-a-step-smaller pairing, and it respects Butterick’s warning not to set reading text in mono.
- Opinion: do not paste SF’s tracking table onto another family. The transferable rule is that tracking is size-dependent and the face should supply it. If the chosen font has an opsz axis, set `font-optical-sizing: auto` and do not hand-pick a “Text” or “Display” cut. If it does not, follow the foundry’s size cuts. Positive tracking belongs on very small UI labels; negative tracking is a display decision for that specific face, not a house default of −0.02em on every heading.
- Opinion: all-caps category labels and overlines get Butterick’s 0.05–0.12em. Body and table prose get 0. Prices, stock counts, and columns of part numbers get `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums`. Part numbers that mix letters and digits also get `slashed-zero` when the font has it, because O and 0 are a real misread in component codes. A part number inside a sentence can stay proportional lining so it does not look like a ripped-out table cell. Oldstyle figures are a prose choice; they are a poor default for a catalog, where digits are data.
- Opinion: do not generate the scale from a single ratio. Geist and Apple both publish named roles. A catalog scale can be something like display, title, heading, body, label, caption, and mono, each with size, line-height, weight, and tracking locked together. Reading passages (about, product story) follow Butterick: at least 15px, line-height about 1.2–1.45, and a measure of 45–90 characters. Spec tables are not paragraphs; the measure rule applies to the prose column, not to every cell. Dense labels may be smaller than body, but they should be tested against a floor rather than shrunk until the grid looks tidy. Apple’s 11 pt iOS minimum is a native-app floor, not a web pixel value.
- Opinion: two weights for UI text (regular and a medium/semibold) plus italic for emphasis is enough. A third weight needs a job. Light and thin weights fail Apple’s legibility rule at catalog sizes.

### Gaps

- The official Apple typography URL did not return body HTML in fetch. Specifications above come from a mirror that reproduces the HIG, including a December 2025 changelog. An iOS-specific tracking table was not in the mirrored macOS/tvOS tables.
- Stripe’s fetched style page showed the `font` property and the example `heading`, then omitted the rest of the style list. Do not treat “subheading, body, caption” as confirmed from this pass.
- No 2023–2026 primary source in this set prescribes a mathematical modular scale (a single ratio such as 1.25) for product UI. Classic modular-scale essays were not re-fetched.
- No foundry specimen for a specific shop typeface was chosen. Pairing advice here is structural. A real pairing still has to be checked in the chosen fonts at 13–16px and at display size.
- Linear has no public type spec. Third-party pixel measurements should not be implemented as if they were Linear’s system.

## What spacing and alignment systems do serious product teams publish, and where do they break the grid on purpose?

### Takeaway

Use a 4px base with an 8px step for component padding, gaps, and control heights. Align type to a 4px baseline, and allow type to leave that grid when it is optically centered in a control. Name the steps. Break the grid for centering, for keylines that are not column edges, and for hairlines or icons that are too small for 8px. Do not use a decorative column grid as the catalog layout.

### Cited Findings

- Material’s spacing method: components align to an 8dp square grid on mobile, tablet, and desktop. Type aligns to a 4dp baseline. Type may sit off the 4dp grid when it is centered inside a component such as a button or list item; it can still look vertically centered. Toolbar iconography aligns to 4dp. Padding is measured in 8dp or 4dp increments and does not have to span the whole layout. Component heights (app bar, list) align to 8dp; widths are often unspecified because they follow the viewport. Keylines are vertical alignment guides for elements that do not sit on the layout grid; they are measured from the screen edge in 8dp increments and can change by breakpoint. — [Spacing methods, Material Design](https://m2.material.io/design/layout/spacing-methods.html)
- Older Material metrics: all components on an 8dp grid; toolbar icons on 4dp; type on a 4dp baseline. Touch targets at least 48×48dp, with 8dp or more between them. — [Metrics and keylines, Material Design](https://m1.material.io/layout/metrics-keylines.html)
- Current Android guidance: if you use a baseline grid, stick to measurements of 4 and 8. An 8dp grid is for layout, components, and spacing. Icons, type, and some elements inside components align to 4dp. Font sizes are specified in sp so they follow the user’s text-size preference. — [Grids and units, Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/mobile/guides/layout-and-content/grids-and-units)
- Stripe Apps spacing tokens, documented as the values for margin, padding, and gap: `0` = 0, `xxsmall` = 2px, `xsmall` = 4px, `small` = 8px, `medium` = 16px, `large` = 24px, `xlarge` = 32px, `xxlarge` = 48px. Layout is stacks (`x`, `y`, `z`) with `gap`, `alignX`, `alignY` (including `baseline`), and `distribute: space-between`. Widths are fractions (halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, sixths, twelfths) or `fill`, `min`, `max`, `fit`. The 2px step is the published exception under the 4px step. — [Style your app, Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/style)
- Stripe separates containers that may be custom-styled (`Box`, `Inline`) from components with preset styles. Some components style themselves and cannot be overridden, specifically so behavior stays recognizable. — [Style your app, Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/style)
- Vercel’s Geist Grid is a visible cell-and-guide layout, not a general spacing scale. The docs say to use it for two-dimensional layouts on marketing pages, docs landings, and feature breakdowns “where the rule lines and cell borders are part of the design.” For ordinary cards and lists, use a plain CSS grid; Grid is “overkill when guides aren’t visible.” Do not nest Grids more than one level. Set columns and rows at all three breakpoints. Hide row or column guides only when hiding them improves clarity; hiding both means a plain grid was the right tool. Guides are decorative (`aria-hidden`). Custom borders on guides must still meet 3:1. — [Geist Grid](https://vercel.com/geist/grid)
- Geist color steps encode spacing-adjacent UI states as roles (default, hover, active background; default, hover, active border) rather than as one-off pixels. See the color section.
- Apple’s typography guidance is the vertical-rhythm rule they publish for text: loose leading in wide or long text, tight leading only in a short constrained row, and never tight leading across three or more lines. Layout must reflow when text size increases: stack secondary items under primary text, and drop columns, rather than truncating. — [Typography, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- Butterick’s vertical rhythm for prose is line-height 120–145% and a 45–90 character measure, set with page margins rather than special indents. Paragraph separation is either a first-line indent or space between paragraphs, not both. — [Summary of key rules](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html); [Line length](https://practicaltypography.com/line-length.html)
- Linear’s March 2024 redesign note says the sidebar, tabs, headers, and panels were adjusted to reduce visual noise, maintain visual alignment, and increase hierarchy and density. It does not publish a spacing token table. — [How we redesigned the Linear UI (part II)](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)

### Inferences

- Opinion, for this shop: a spacing scale of 0, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64. The 2px step is only for hairline offsets, focus rings, and icon optical alignment (Stripe already publishes 2px). Everything else snaps to 4 or 8. Component padding and gaps use the named tokens. Raw `13px` or `18px` in a component is a defect.
- Opinion: catalog rows align part number, description, price, and stock on a shared baseline or on a shared center line, and the choice is consistent. Prices in a column are right-aligned or decimal-aligned; part numbers are left-aligned in tabular figures. That is the alignment people use to compare parts. Centered cards fight that job.
- Opinion: break the grid on purpose in four cases only. (1) Optically center a label in a button or row when the baseline would look low, as Material allows. (2) A 1px keyline or divider, which cannot be an 8px object. (3) Icon artwork that needs a pixel of optical shift inside its box. (4) A full-bleed product photo whose crop is about the object, not the column. Document the exception next to the component. Do not “break the grid” to make a hero look designed.
- Opinion: one content width for reading (about 65–75 characters, inside Butterick’s 45–90), and a wider width for the catalog table. The table is the product. Do not cap a parts table at a novel measure and then wrap every spec into a card. Horizontal padding on the page can follow Material’s idea of a keyline from the edge (a token such as 16px on small screens and 24px on large), not a new number per page.
- Opinion: vertical rhythm between sections uses the larger tokens (32, 48, 64). Inside a spec block, rhythm is the type’s line-height, not a second competing grid. When Dynamic Type or a 200% browser zoom (WCAG 1.4.4) makes a row overflow, stack the price under the name. Do not shrink the type to save the row.

### Gaps

- Material 3’s grids-and-spacing pages returned only titles in fetch. Spacing rules above are from Material 2’s spacing-methods page and from current Android developer docs, which still state the 4/8 rule.
- No official Apple web container-width or 8px spacing spec was retrieved. Apple’s layout HIG was not successfully fetched.
- No official Linear or Vercel spacing-token table (4/8/16…) was retrieved. Vercel’s public Grid page is a marketing guide component. Stripe’s scale is the only first-party commerce-adjacent spacing token list confirmed in full.
- A single “correct” max width in pixels for a parts table was not published by these teams.

## What motion principles are documented, and which motions belong on a catalog versus a marketing site?

### Takeaway

Catalog motion is a short, interruptible response to an action: hover, press, add-to-cart, open a drawer. It uses ease-out or a critically damped spring, stays under about half a second, and never bounces. Marketing motion can be slower and more staged, and it still yields to reduced motion. Anything that moves by itself needs a pause control.

### Cited Findings

- WWDC18 “Designing Fluid Interfaces” (Chan Karunamuni, Nathan de Vries, Marcos Alonso): an interface feels fluid when it behaves like an extension of the person using it. Principles they name: instant response (latency is felt immediately); interruption and redirection at any moment, including mid-transition; spatial consistency (something leaves and returns on a symmetric path); motion should hint toward the gesture’s outcome; interactions stay lightweight while the result can be amplified; boundaries rubber-band instead of hitting a hard stop; smoothness depends on the change between frames, not only on frame rate; prefer continuous behavior over a timed animation that locks the user out until it finishes. — [Designing Fluid Interfaces, WWDC18](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Same talk, on springs: they avoid the word duration for elastic behavior because a spring is always interruptible. Designer-facing controls are damping and response (damping ratio and frequency response), with mass, stiffness, and damping kept underneath. Start at 100% damping, meaning no overshoot, so motion does not get in the way of a tool. “A spring doesn’t need to overshoot.” Use a little overshoot only when the gesture itself has momentum in that direction (their example: 80% damping on a swipe dismiss, 100% on a tap that has no momentum). Bounce can also teach that a control needs a firmer press. Behaviors should feel like one family so the app stays in character. Tools “require us to not be in the way with introducing unnecessary motion.” — [Designing Fluid Interfaces, WWDC18](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- WWDC23: springs are the default SwiftUI animation. A spring does not have a clean end; settling duration is when the motion is small enough to remove, and it is unpredictable. The duration parameter is a perceptual duration, not the settling time. Do not wait on settling duration for user-facing changes. Bounce is parameterized from −1.0 to 1.0. Presets exist and are meant to be tuned. — [Animate with springs, WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/)
- Apple’s Reduced Motion evaluation criteria: detect the system setting rather than making the person set it again inside the app. Disable or replace parallax, animated blur, depth of field, multi-axis motion, multi-speed motion, spinning, and vortex effects. Stop or control auto-advancing carousels and other ongoing motion. Purely stylistic animation should stop under reduced motion. Motion that conveys meaning (a status change, or the fact that a view is a child of the previous view) should not vanish; replace it with a dissolve, highlight, or color shift, or otherwise reduce full-screen movement. — [Reduced Motion evaluation criteria, Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/reduced-motion-evaluation-criteria/)
- web.dev: `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` means the person asked the OS to minimize animation, preferably to the point where non-essential movement is removed. `no-preference` means they have not set it. OS labels differ (macOS “Reduce motion,” Android “Remove animations,” Windows “Show animations” on by default). The article says even animations under WCAG’s five-second threshold should be treated cautiously, and that the reliable pattern is to give people who asked for reduced motion control over animation. — [prefers-reduced-motion, web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion); [Animation and motion, web.dev](https://web.dev/learn/accessibility/motion)
- WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A): if something moves, blinks, or scrolls, starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds, and is parallel with other content, the user must be able to pause, stop, or hide it unless the movement is essential. The same applies to auto-updating information. A preload animation can be essential if interaction cannot happen during it and hiding progress would make the interface seem broken. This criterion is non-interference: failing it fails the page. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- WCAG 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (Level AAA): motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled unless it is essential to the function or the information. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- web.dev on easing: ease-out is the default for UI because it starts quickly and slows at the end. Ease-out and ease-in durations about 200–500ms. Bounce and elastic eases about 800–1200ms, used sparingly, and not at all if the product is not meant to feel playful. “If your project isn’t intended to be fun, then don’t have elements bouncing around the UI.” — [Choosing the right easing, web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/choosing-the-right-easing)
- Material Design 3’s easing-and-duration page says the expressive update moved components to a spring-based motion physics system, and that the older easing-and-duration system is no longer maintained, though it remains available for transitions. The still-published starting pairs include: emphasized 500ms when the element begins and ends on screen; emphasized decelerate 400ms to enter; emphasized accelerate 200ms to exit; standard 300ms on screen; standard decelerate 250ms to enter; standard accelerate 200ms to exit. Duration tokens run from short1 50ms through short4 200ms (small utility transitions; selection controls cited at 200ms), medium 250–400ms, long 450–600ms (a card expanding to full screen cited at 500ms), and extra-long 700–1000ms, “usually used for ambient transitions that don’t involve user input” (an auto-advancing carousel cited at 1000ms). — [Easing and duration, Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration); [tokens and specs](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration/tokens-specs)
- Material Web’s generated token file (v0.192, copyright 2023) records the cubic beziers: standard and emphasized `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`; emphasized decelerate `cubic-bezier(0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1)`; emphasized accelerate `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 0.8, 0.15)`; standard decelerate `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0, 1)`; standard accelerate `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 1, 1)`; linear `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 1, 1)`. — [Material Web motion tokens](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/material-components/material-web/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-motion.scss)
- Older Material guidance, still useful as a desktop-versus-mobile distinction: desktop transitions should be faster and simpler than mobile, about 150–200ms, because they are seen often and dropped frames are less forgivable if the animation is long. Mobile transitions were described around 300ms, with exits shorter than entrances, and transitions over 400ms at risk of feeling slow. Duration should change with distance traveled. — [Duration and easing, Material Design (classic)](https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html)
- Vercel’s design-engineering post treats “no dropped frames,” cross-browser consistency, and accessibility as part of the quality bar, and lists “delighters” in the dashboard as a separate, intentional class of work from ordinary features. — [Design Engineering at Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/design-engineering-at-vercel)
- Vercel Ship 2025 is explicitly a marketing/conference site: ferrofluid-style 3D, scroll-linked zoom via Motion’s `useTransform`, sticky sections. That is a campaign surface, not the dashboard’s everyday UI. — [Designing and building the Vercel Ship conference platform](https://vercel.com/blog/designing-and-building-the-vercel-ship-conference-platform)
- Geist Grid’s own docs separate “marketing pages, docs landing pages, and feature breakdowns” from plain card and list layouts. — [Geist Grid](https://vercel.com/geist/grid)

### Inferences

- Opinion: the catalog (home grid or table, category, product, cart, checkout) gets only functional motion. Hover and press: opacity or background token change, about 100–150ms, ease-out. Drawer, menu, and dialog: about 200–250ms, ease-out or a spring with no bounce, interruptible if the person hits Escape or the scrim. Add-to-cart: a short confirmation (a check or a count change), not a flying product thumbnail across the page. Quantity changes should not animate the digits through intermediate values; tabular figures already keep them from jittering.
- Opinion: the marketing exceptions are the home introduction and empty editorial pages, and even there the motion is one entrance, not a system of scroll effects. No marquee, no gradient drift, no parallax, no autoplaying carousel. If a carousel is ever added, WCAG 2.2.2 requires a pause if it runs longer than five seconds beside other content.
- Opinion: under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, drop transforms and parallax. Keep instantaneous state changes or a short opacity cross-fade so the person can still tell that a panel opened or an item was added (Apple’s rule that meaningful motion is replaced, not deleted). Do not gate this behind a custom toggle that defaults to on.
- Opinion: one easing family for the whole shop. web.dev’s “if it isn’t fun, don’t bounce” fits a parts catalog. A single overshoot on a cart badge would break the “instrument” character the way Apple says mixed behaviors break a tool.
- Opinion: do not animate layout properties (`width`, `height`, `top`, `margin`) on catalog rows. Those are the interactions most likely to drop frames, which Vercel treats as a quality failure. Animate `transform` and `opacity`, or do not animate.

### Gaps

- Linear has not published an official motion spec in the sources found. Third-party notes of 100ms, 160ms, and 400ms on linear.app are observations, not a Linear standard.
- Apple does not publish a web CSS duration table. The spring guidance is from native WWDC talks. Mapping “100% damping” onto a CSS cubic-bezier is an approximation; the honest web equivalent is ease-out with no bounce.
- Material’s expressive spring presets (the replacement for the duration tokens) were not retrieved as a parameter table, only the statement that teams should migrate to springs and that the duration system is unmaintained.
- No source in this set measured catalog-task time against animation length. The catalog-versus-marketing split is an inference from Apple’s tool guidance, Geist’s marketing-versus-plain-grid split, and Vercel Ship being a separate marketing build.

## How should color be structured as tokens so a parts catalog can feel precise and calm? What contrast ratios are required?

### Takeaway

Name colors by role — surface, ink, line, one accent, and a separate status set — and bind each text role to the surface it sits on. Meet WCAG AA as a floor: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text and for UI boundaries that carry meaning. Status must not depend on hue alone. Do not adopt Material’s or Geist’s hues; copy the role structure only.

### Cited Findings

- WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), Level AA: text and images of text at least 4.5:1. Large-scale text at least 3:1. Incidental text (inactive controls, pure decoration, text that is not visible, text that is part of a picture with other significant content) and logotypes have no contrast requirement. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.4.6 Contrast (Enhanced), Level AAA: 7:1 for normal text, 4.5:1 for large-scale text, with the same incidental and logo exceptions. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, Level AA: at least 3:1 against adjacent colors for visual information needed to identify a UI component and its states (inactive components and browser-default appearances excepted), and for parts of graphics required to understand the content. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- WCAG 2.2 Success Criterion 1.4.1 Use of Color, Level A: color is not the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response, or distinguishing a visual element. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- MDN restates the same ratios and defines large text as at least 18pt, or 14pt bold. It recommends aiming at AAA and at least meeting AA, and points at the WebAIM contrast checker. — [Color contrast, MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/Guides/Understanding_WCAG/Perceivable/Color_contrast)
- WCAG’s contrast ratio is `(L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)`. Authors do not control anti-aliasing, so text contrast can be evaluated with anti-aliasing off. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- Geist’s public color system is a set of scales (`backgrounds`, `gray`, `gray-alpha`, `blue`, `red`, `amber`, `green`, `teal`, `purple`, `pink`), each non-background scale having steps 100–1000 with a job, not a decorative shade number: 100 default background, 200 hover background, 300 active background, 400 default border, 500 hover border, 600 active border, 700 high-contrast background, 800 hover high-contrast background, 900 secondary text and icons, 1000 primary text and icons. Background 1 is the default page/element background; Background 2 is a rare secondary differentiation. Use solid gray for text, icons, fills, borders, and dividers. Use gray-alpha only when the background must show through (shadows, overlays). Draw each border once; overlapping translucent borders make intersections louder. Shadows stay translucent so they do not leave an opaque halo. — [Geist Colors](https://vercel.com/geist/colors)
- Material 3’s `ColorScheme`, as documented for Jetpack Compose, is a role set: `surface` for component surfaces (cards, sheets, menus); a ladder of `surfaceContainerLowest` through `surfaceContainerHighest` for emphasis; `onSurface` for text and icons on surface; `onSurfaceVariant` for lower-emphasis content on surface; `outline` as a subtle boundary color that “adds contrast for accessibility purposes” (wording on the Material color-role summary; the Compose reference shortens this to “subtle color used for boundaries”); `outlineVariant` for decorative boundaries “when strong contrast is not required”; `primary` / `onPrimary` / `primaryContainer` / `onPrimaryContainer` for the main accent and the content on it; `error` / `onError` / `errorContainer` / `onErrorContainer` for errors such as invalid input. `surfaceTint` is an elevation overlay and is called out here only so a catalog can refuse it. The scheme exists in light and dark. — [ColorScheme, Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/ColorScheme)
- Material’s design-token overview describes three layers: reference tokens (raw values), system tokens (decisions and roles: color, typography, elevation, shape), and component tokens (the property on a specific component). The fetched page did not return the article body; this sentence is from the search snippet of that URL. — [Design tokens, Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/foundations/design-tokens)
- Stripe Apps color is also role-shaped, even though the fetched page omitted the full enums. Documented properties: `backgroundColor` (example value `container`), `keyline` (example value `neutral`), and `color` / `fill` for text and icons (example value `success` for a status, `primary` for ordinary text). Icons inherit surrounding text unless `fill` overrides them. — [Style your app, Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/style)
- Apple, on visionOS text, says to maximize contrast between text and its container, and not to rely on shadows to rescue low-contrast type. The principle is general even though the platform is not the web. — [Typography, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)

### Inferences

- Opinion: the shop’s token set, with no hues chosen yet:
  - Surface: `surface` (page), `surface-raised` (table header, cart drawer, input), `surface-sunken` (a rare inset, such as a spec well). Two or three surfaces, not five.
  - Ink: `ink` (primary text, at least 4.5:1 on `surface` and on `surface-raised`), `ink-muted` (secondary spec labels; still 4.5:1 if the text is normal size), `ink-faint` only for inactive text, which WCAG exempts, and never for a price or a part number.
  - Line: `line` (row rules, input borders) at 3:1 against the adjacent surface when the line is how you see the control; `line-subtle` for decorative row rules inside a table that already has text alignment. Geist’s warning applies: one solid border, not stacked translucent hairlines.
  - Accent: one accent, used for the primary action and for the current link or selected row. It is not a second text color for headings. `on-accent` is its own token and must clear 4.5:1 on the accent fill.
  - Status, separate from accent: `success`, `warning`, `danger`, `info`, each with an `on-*` for text on a filled chip and a muted container (`danger-container` plus `ink`) for inline messages. Stock, error, and “in cart” states also use a word or an icon. Color alone fails WCAG 1.4.1.
- Opinion: calm and precise comes from a near-neutral surface and ink, a quiet line, and the accent appearing only where a decision is. Status colors are dull enough to sit in a table without shouting, and still pass contrast when used as text. Do not use tonal elevation (Material’s `surfaceTint`) to separate rows; use spacing and one line token.
- Opinion: check pairs, not swatches. The required pairs are ink on surface, ink on surface-raised, ink-muted on both, on-accent on accent, each status text on its container, and line against surface for controls. Large prices (18pt / 24px, or 14pt bold) may use the 3:1 large-text floor, but a catalog price is doing a job that benefits from the 4.5:1 floor anyway. Aim body ink and price ink at AAA 7:1 if the palette allows; that is MDN’s recommendation, not the legal floor.
- Opinion: light and dark, if both exist, are two assignments of the same roles, not a second palette with new meanings. Borders get slightly stronger in dark mode because 1px lines lose contrast. Do not invert the accent into a neon on near-black just because dark mode is on. That pattern is called out in the next section.

### Gaps

- Stripe’s full color-token enum did not survive fetch. Only the property model and the example values `container`, `neutral`, `primary`, and `success` were confirmed.
- Geist publishes the step-to-role mapping but the fetched page did not include the literal hex or P3 values. That is fine: this shop should not copy them.
- Material’s color-roles marketing page did not render. Role definitions are from the Compose `ColorScheme` reference.
- No source here gives a contrast number for “muted but still readable” secondary text beyond WCAG. If muted text is normal-size text, it is 4.5:1. There is no AA exception for “de-emphasized but still informative” spec labels.
- A specific shop palette was intentionally not chosen.

## What frontend architecture practices keep a small Astro or React commerce UI maintainable, and what should be refused?

### Takeaway

Put decisions in tokens, lock text and space into named styles, and let components expose a few variants. Keep the catalog content as structured part data. Refuse one-off pixel values, arbitrary fonts, decorative motion, and the generic AI looks listed below.

### Cited Findings

- Stripe Apps: design tokens are exposed through TypeScript so the editor autocompletes legal values. Arbitrary font faces are not allowed; typography is a `font` token. Spacing, color, and layout are tokens. Components other than `Box` and `Inline` have preset styles. Some components style themselves from their behavior and cannot be restyled, “to avoid confusion.” Buttons and similar components offer a few styles through a prop. Icons accept color only through `fill`. — [Style your app, Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/stripe-apps/style)
- Geist typography classes are the implementation of “a style is a bundle”: one class sets size, line-height, tracking, and weight. Button text styles exist only for button components. — [Geist Typography](https://vercel.com/geist/typography)
- Material’s token model, from the search snippet of the foundations page: reference tokens (raw values), system tokens (roles), component tokens (a component’s assigned properties). — [Design tokens, Material Design 3](https://m3.material.io/foundations/design-tokens)
- Vercel on design systems and generated UI: a design system is tokens, patterns, and structure (color, type, spacing, motion, tone). Prototypes that “feel real” need that system, not only styled components. Their recommended start is to apply existing tokens (colors, radius, spacing, fonts) as the constraint on generation. Geist is the example of foundations first, shared components second. — [AI-powered prototyping with design systems](https://vercel.com/blog/ai-powered-prototyping-with-design-systems)
- Vercel design engineering: quality includes polished interaction, no dropped frames, cross-browser consistency, and accessibility. “Delighters” are a named, separate investment, not the default for every control. Ship 2025’s reusable UI was themeable components (shadcn, Geist, and `cva`) so events could vary without restyling from scratch. — [Design Engineering at Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/design-engineering-at-vercel); [Vercel Ship](https://vercel.com/blog/designing-and-building-the-vercel-ship-conference-platform)
- Apple: use text styles rather than ad hoc sizes, keep hierarchy when size changes, and do not truncate content the person came to read. Custom fonts must still participate in text resizing and bold-text accessibility. — [Typography, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography); [Get started with Dynamic Type, WWDC24](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10074/)
- WCAG 1.4.4 Resize Text (Level AA): except captions and images of text, text can be resized to 200% without loss of content or function. — [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)
- Linear’s redesign write-up frames craft as reducing noise and keeping alignment so the tool can get denser without falling apart. It is a product essay, not a component-API spec. — [How we redesigned the Linear UI (part II)](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)

### Inferences

- Opinion: three layers, matching Material’s structure and Stripe’s enforcement. Reference tokens are the raw palette, type sizes, and the spacing steps, and they are not used in components. System tokens are the roles (`color.ink`, `color.surface`, `space.16`, `font.label`). Component tokens, or a small set of variants, are the only API a page should touch (`Button` variant `primary` or `quiet`, `Price` which always sets tabular lining figures).
- Opinion, component API: variants are a closed union (`primary`, `quiet`, `danger`), sizes are `sm` and `md`, and there is no `className` escape hatch for color or spacing on first-party components. A page that needs a new look adds a variant or it does not ship. This is Stripe’s “preset styles, limited CSS” translated to a small Astro/React shop.
- Opinion, content shape: a part is data — part number, manufacturer, short name, price, currency, stock state, package, and a list of spec rows `{ label, value, unit }` — not a blob of HTML. The product page, the table, and the cart all read the same fields. Spec labels come from the data, so column alignment and tabular figures are possible. Marketing copy is a separate optional field with the reading measure applied. Do not encode hierarchy by wrapping specs in hand-built cards per product.
- Opinion, what to refuse in implementation:
  - A magic number for margin, padding, gap, type size, radius, or duration that is not a token.
  - A second accent, a gradient fill, or a shadow used to create hierarchy that spacing and one line token could create.
  - Decorative animation: looping gradients, scroll-scrubbed heroes, bounces, marquees. Functional motion follows the catalog rules above.
  - Font stacks that are only Inter, or only the OS UI font, with no other typographic decision. See the next section.
  - Per-page CSS that restyles a shared component for one section.
- Opinion: Astro pages should stay thin. Interactive islands (cart, quantity, mobile nav) are the only client components. Tokens live in one CSS file as custom properties. If a value is used twice, it is a token. If it is used once and it is not on the scale, it is wrong.

### Gaps

- No Astro-specific design-token case study from these companies was found. The architecture above is translated from Stripe Apps, Geist, and Material.
- Vercel’s `cva` mention is from a conference-platform post, not a published component-API standard for commerce.
- “Content shape” for a parts catalog is an inference. None of the cited design systems define a product-spec schema.

## Which generic AI-looking defaults should a parts catalog refuse?

### Takeaway

Refuse the six looks named in the brief. They signal a template, not an instrument. The replacement is one deliberate type pairing, neutral surfaces, one accent, tabular data, and almost no decoration.

### Cited Findings

- No Apple, Linear, Stripe, Vercel, Material, or WCAG document was found that catalogs these six looks as “AI defaults.” The list comes from the research brief. What the primary sources do say, and what makes those looks fail, is below.
- Apple: too many typefaces obscure hierarchy and feel poorly designed. Light weights harm legibility. Unnecessary motion gets in the way of a tool. — [Typography, Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography); [Designing Fluid Interfaces, WWDC18](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/)
- Butterick: the easiest improvement is a professional font, and system fonts (he names Times New Roman and Arial) are something to avoid. He is writing about documents, not design-tool defaults, but the claim “a default face with no decision is not craft” is his. — [Summary of key rules, Butterick](https://practicaltypography.com/summary-of-key-rules.html)
- Geist: the visible guide-grid is for marketing features where the lines are the design. Ordinary cards should not wear that grid. Overlapping translucent borders and opaque shadows are called out as mistakes inside their own system. — [Geist Grid](https://vercel.com/geist/grid); [Geist Colors](https://vercel.com/geist/colors)
- Vercel’s prototyping post: generated UI only feels specific when tokens constrain it. Unconstrained generation is how a page collapses to whatever the model has seen most. — [AI-powered prototyping with design systems](https://vercel.com/blog/ai-powered-prototyping-with-design-systems)
- web.dev: bounce and elastic motion do not belong on a product that is not trying to be playful. — [Choosing the right easing, web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/choosing-the-right-easing)

### Inferences

- Opinion, refuse these by name. They are not documented as a formal anti-pattern list; they are the brief’s list, and they conflict with the rules above.
  - Cream paper with terracotta (or any single clay/brown accent on a warm off-white page). It reads as a lifestyle template. A parts catalog can be warm or cool, but the surface should be quiet and the accent has to survive a 4.5:1 check on its text, which this pairing usually fails by being fashionable rather than measured.
  - Near-black page with one acid accent (electric violet, laser green, hot magenta) as the only color decision. High contrast is good; a neon accent on charcoal is a costume. Geist’s model is a neutral scale with a clear text and border job, not a black field plus one glowing hue.
  - Broadsheet hairline layouts with no editorial reason: huge margins, a three-line drop cap, and 1px rules everywhere imitating a newspaper. Hairlines are allowed as the table’s `line` token. They are not a personality. Geist explicitly tells you not to use the decorative grid when you are just laying out cards.
  - Inter, or the raw system stack, as the only typographic choice. Inter is a competent UI face. Using it because it was the default, with default tracking and a default 16/24 body, is no point of view. Butterick’s rule is to choose a font on purpose. The shop needs a stated reason for the text face and a mono that is visibly for part numbers.
  - Identical card grids: same radius, same shadow, same padding, one image and two lines, repeated. A catalog of components is a table or a dense list with aligned part numbers and prices. Cards are for a small set of featured parts, not for every SKU. Vercel’s own grid docs say plain layout, not a designed grid, when the guides are not the point.
  - Gradient-mesh heroes, soft blobs, and animated color fields behind a headline. They are decoration. They also fight reduced-motion and they compete with the product photo, which is the thing a buyer is trying to see.
- Opinion: the one memorable signature should be typographic and structural. Candidates that fit the research: a distinctive but highly legible text face at a disciplined size; part numbers always in the mono with tabular lining figures and a slashed zero; prices always in tabular figures, aligned. That signature shows up on every row, which is more memorable than a hero. It should appear once per view as a system, not as a new decoration on each section.
- Opinion: radius stays small and constant (a token, 0 or 4px for rows and inputs). Shadows are reserved for overlays that actually float (cart drawer, menu), and they are translucent, per Geist. If a surface change can do the job, do not add a shadow.

### Gaps

- There is no primary 2023–2026 study, from the teams in this brief, that measures how often these six looks appear in generated sites. The refusal list is judgment, anchored to the cited craft rules.
- “Inter is the AI default” was not verified with a font-usage census in this pass. The implementation rule does not depend on that census: an unchosen default face is already a Butterick failure, whether or not it is statistically common.
